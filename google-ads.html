Five problems, one cause each.

Most Google Ads accounts don't need more budget. They need someone to read them. Each of these has a specific, findable cause, and each one is visible in an afternoon if someone actually looks.

“We're paying for searches that will never buy.”

Search terms are read every day and negatives proposed with the wasted spend attached, so you can see what each one cost before you approve it. Job seekers, DIY-ers, the wrong city: the list that stops it, written and ready.

“The clicks come in and nothing happens.”

Almost always the page. A homepage gives a visitor nine things to do; a landing page gives them one. We build the page the ad lands on, for the search it came from.

“Our cost per lead looks fine and the leads are junk.”

Mark a lead sold or lost. That verdict goes back to Google as an offline conversion, so bidding optimises toward customers instead of form fills.

“Budget runs out on the 20th.”

Pacing is watched against the month, and spend limits are hard limits in software rather than a note in someone's calendar.

“Nobody can tell us what changed or why.”

Every change carries its evidence, its approver and its timestamp, and the record stays in your account's history. Ask any agency for its change history. If nobody can produce it, that's the answer.

How we build an account

Read the market before you spend on it.

A new account starts small and structured to learn, not to impress. The point of the first weeks is clean signal: which searches in which towns actually produce customers, at what cost. Then the money follows the evidence.

Week one

One campaign per location, $30 to $50 a day

Each town or facility gets its own campaign and budget, so its impression share and conversion rate are readable on their own. Broad match on the non-brand terms, so the search-terms report fills with what people actually type.

Week one

Brand carved out, exact match

Every spelling of your name runs in its own cheap campaign, and a brand negative list sits on everything else. Your non-brand numbers stay honest instead of being flattered by people who were already looking for you.

Week one

Ads written in full, not placeholders

Fifteen headlines and four descriptions per ad group, with the searcher's keyword and city inserted, every character counted. Claims come from your site: real prices, real hours, real promotions, nothing invented.

Weeks two to four

Tracking proven before bidding is trusted

Conversion tracking is wrong in more accounts than it's right. We verify what every conversion action fires on, check what's published versus what's live in Tag Manager, and only then let Google's bidding learn from it.

Month two onward

Budget follows the evidence

The locations and themes that produce customers get more. The ones that don't get cut, with the dollars that justify it. Kalamazoo Mortgage went from $30 a day to $100 this way, and stopped there only because the sales team was full.

Always

The landing page, built for the search

Written for one search, built to be read on a phone, one form and one number, tracked end to end. On its own it's a $2,500 build. It's included, on your domain, and it stays yours.

Every day, not every month

What gets checked while you're running the business.

Each check has one job and a schedule. The software reads your account, writes up what it found with the dollars at stake, and drafts the fix. It cannot apply anything. Jordan reads what it wrote and decides.

  • Search-term harvest. Every new query, sorted into keywords worth adding and negatives worth adding, each with its spend.
  • Budget pacing. Whether the month runs dry on the 20th, and whether money sits in campaigns converting worse than the ones starved of it.
  • Waste cutter. Terms, placements and hours that spend and never convert, flagged the day it starts, not the month after.
  • Ads optimiser. Click-through, conversion rate and cost per lead by ad and ad group; tired copy proposed for replacement.
  • Tracking audit. Tags edited but never published, conversions counting page views, calls nobody is recording.
  • Competitive research. Who outranks you for the searches you already win, and what they're saying.
Nothing moves without a name on it

Every change is a draft until a person signs.

A proposed change lands in one queue with its evidence and the money at stake. Jordan or Ted approves it or rejects it; neither can approve their own proposal. Spend limits are enforced in code and can't be overridden, even by an approver. You can pause everything with one switch.

Email us at 9:14 about the ad quoting the wrong price, and the fix is drafted from the live account by 9:15 and applied by a person by 9:31. An illustration of the path, not a promise of the minutes.

Leads teach the ads

Google learns from the leads you'd actually call back.

Left alone, Google optimises for whatever it's told is a conversion, which is usually a form fill, and form fills include the tire-kickers, the job seekers and the wrong city. We close the loop: when a lead is marked sold or lost, that verdict goes back to Google as an offline conversion, and the bidding moves toward the searches that produce customers.

  • Calls and forms tracked per campaign and keyword, verified before they're reported
  • Sold-or-lost sent back from your CRM, your booking tool, or our app
  • Reported as customers and cost per customer, not clicks and impressions

Questions

Asked on almost every first call about Google Ads.

Do I need to be running Google Ads already?

No. If you're not, we look at what people actually search for in your market, what those clicks cost, and whether ads are even the right first move. Sometimes the answer is that your site or your local listings need the work first, and we'll say so.

What's in the audit, exactly?

The search terms that spent and never converted, with the dollars next to each one. The negatives that stop it, already written. Whatever is broken in your conversion tracking. Budget pacing. Ad copy that has run unchanged for months. Then a short ranked list of what we'd change first, whoever ends up making the changes. We go through it with you live, and you keep all of it.

How long before we see anything?

Audit findings come back within a few days of read-only access. Once an account is live, the first weeks are spent building enough conversion history for bidding to have something to learn from, and we'll tell you plainly where that stands rather than declare victory early.

Will you contact our current agency?

No. Read-only access is something you grant and revoke yourself in Google Ads, and it doesn't notify them. If the audit says they're doing fine, we'll say so, in writing.

Who owns the ad account?

You do. It stays in your name, and you can revoke our access any time without losing the history. We don't run clients out of our own account.

How is it priced?

A monthly fee based on ad spend, quoted after the audit once we know what's actually there. Month to month, no contract.
