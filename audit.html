Why it's worth the form

Most accounts we open are leaking money.

Not because anyone's lazy. Because nobody reads the account every day, and the leaks are small, specific and dull: a search term that never bought, a conversion that counts page views, a budget that quietly ran out on the 20th. These are the kinds of findings an audit turns up, from real accounts.

of a twelve-month engagement with no tracking on the booking flow at all. About $75,000 spent, reported as page views. BioFuse, before we opened it.

What the audit looks for

Six places the money hides, and two more on the site.

The software pulls every search term and every conversion action, which is the part software is good at. A person reads the result and decides what it means. You get the raw rows, not a chart of them.

Search terms that spend and never convert

Every query eating budget with nothing to show, each with its spend next to it. Usually the first time anyone has shown you this.

Missing negatives

Job seekers, DIY-ers, the wrong city. The negatives you need, drafted and ready to add, whether by you, by us, or by the agency you already have.

Conversion tracking that quietly broke

A tag changed but never published, a form that stopped firing, a conversion counting page views. It looks exactly like a campaign that stopped working.

Budget pacing

Whether the month runs dry on the 20th, and whether money sits in campaigns that convert worse than the ones starved of it.

Campaigns bidding against each other

Overlapping campaigns competing for the same searches, and brand traffic flattering the non-brand numbers.

Tired ad copy

Headlines and descriptions that have run unchanged for months, and what we'd test in their place.

Where the clicks land

Whether paid traffic arrives on a page built to take a lead, or on a homepage with nine other places to go. Speed on a phone, forms that leak, calls nobody is counting.

How you show up when you're not paying

Google Maps, your business profile, and whether ChatGPT names you when someone nearby asks. Listings that disagree with the site, and markets with no page at all.

Said by everyone who eventually switched

If any of these sound familiar, the audit will name the cause.

“The reports look fine but the phone doesn't ring.”

Almost always a conversion action counting something that isn't a lead. We check what every conversion in your account actually fires on before we believe a single number in it.

“We can't tell what they actually do each month.”

Ask for the change history. If nobody can produce a list of what changed, when, and who approved it, that is the answer.

“Our account is in their name, so we can't leave.”

That is the trap, and it's worth knowing before you sign anywhere else. Your accounts stay in your name with us, and the history stays with you if you go.

“Cost per lead keeps climbing and nobody explains it.”

Usually spend drifting to broad terms nobody negated, and a landing page doing none of the work. Both are visible in an afternoon if someone actually looks.

“We asked for the search terms and got a summary.”

You'll get the list. Every term, its spend, and whether it produced anything. The raw rows, not a chart of them.

Questions

Before you send the form.

Will you contact our current agency?

No. We never contact them, and the audit doesn't require telling them anything. Read-only access is something you grant and revoke yourself, and it doesn't notify them.

What if the audit says our agency is doing fine?

Then we'll say so, and you'll have that in writing too. It happens. An account that's genuinely well run is easy to recognise, and telling you otherwise to win the work would be found out in about a month.

Do we have to hire you to get it?

No, and there's nothing to sign. Plenty of people use the write-up as a list of things to ask their existing agency to fix, which is a perfectly good outcome. Some of them come back a few months later.

We're not running ads yet. Is there anything to audit?

Yes: the site, the listings, and the market. We look at what people actually search for where you are, what those clicks cost, and whether ads are even the right first move. Sometimes the answer is that the site or the listings need the work first, and we'll say so.

How long does it take?

Findings come back within a few days of read-only access. The walkthrough is a call whenever suits you, and you keep the write-up at the end of it.

What does it cost if we do hire you?

A monthly fee based on your ad spend and whether we run your site, quoted after the audit once we know what's actually there. Month to month, no contract, and not the cheapest option, because the people doing the work are the people you talk to.
